London, in January

Five nights, four of you, built around what January actually allows: light from about 8:00 to 4:20, so the outdoor things happen early and the city takes over after dark. No sit-down lunches or breakfasts — grab something on the move and spend the daylight on the good stuff.

His largest outdoor show ever: 30 monumental bronzes set through the gardens, plus 70 works on paper, models and small pieces indoors in the Shirley Sherwood Gallery. The categories, and the alternates

Shopping

In the plan

Dover Street Market, Liberty, Coal Drops Yard, Columbia Road, Bath's Milsom Street. Mid-January is the tail of the UK sales.

Dropped to make room: Marylebone High Street / Daunt Books — it's ten minutes from nina on Friday night if you want it; and the Mayfair gallery walk (Hauser & Wirth, Zwirner, Gagosian — all free, all closed Sunday), which fits Wednesday between Soane's and the spa if you skip the shops.

Sculpture

Hepworth & Moore, no Cornwall

Kenwood and Kew's Henry Moore show (Thu), Kettle's Yard (Fri), Tate Britain and Moore's Locking Piece (Sun). That's three Moores and two Hepworths before you count the Tate.

Swap in: The Line, a free outdoor sculpture trail from the Olympic Park to The O2 with Gormley and Gary Hume, in place of the Barbican on Sunday.

Further out — up to three hours

Every realistic day trip, ranked by how long it actually takes to get there. Two are now in the plan — Cambridge on Friday and Bath + the Cotswolds on Saturday. The rest are swaps, or a solo day while the others take it slow in Soho.

Bath + Castle Combe + Lacock — in the plan, Saturday

1h19 from Paddington · villages + spa + architecture

Thermae Bath Spa has an open-air rooftop pool of naturally hot spring water looking over the abbey — steaming in January air, which is the whole point. Open daily 9:00–21:30, over-16s, book a slot.

Around it: the Royal Crescent and the Circus, the Roman Baths, and good shopping on Milsom Street. The Cotswolds proper (Bibury, Stow, Bourton) are a separate 1h30 train to Moreton-in-Marsh and don't combine with Bath in a day; Castle Combe and Lacock are the Cotswold villages that do, 25 minutes from Bath station.

Yorkshire Sculpture Park

~2h20 door to door · the big sculpture day

500 acres with Moore and Hepworth in open parkland, plus The Hepworth Wakefield in a Chipperfield building. YSP is open Tue–Sun, last entry 16:00; £10–12. Wakefield Hepworth Tue–Sun, £13.

07:00 from King's Cross, back by 19:00. A Jenni day, not a group day.

Roche Court + Salisbury

~1h50 · free sculpture park

Sixty acres of Wiltshire parkland with Hepworth, Caro, Gormley, Richard Long and Phyllida Barlow. Free, open seven days, 11–4. Ring to ask what's installed.

Salisbury Cathedral is on the way, and Stonehenge is about twenty minutes from Salisbury if you want to make a full day of it.

Charleston

~1h30 · Victoria to Lewes, then taxi

The Bloomsbury farmhouse where Vanessa Bell and Duncan Grant painted every door, wall and fireplace. Wed–Sun, 10–5, now open year-round. Lewes is a lovely old town for lunch.

Margate

1h27 from St Pancras

Turner Contemporary on the sand (free), the Shell Grotto, a faded seaside old town and good oysters. Cold, windy, very photogenic in winter.

Hauser & Wirth Somerset

~2h15 to Bruton · check before going

A converted farmstead with a Piet Oudolf garden, outdoor sculpture and the Roth Bar. Always free, Wed–Sun. But the galleries close between shows over winter, so confirm what's open in January before committing. The Oudolf field is bare seedheads and structure that time of year.
